01 — Identity

Logo and lockup

A B pressed from two strokes, with the lower bowl in Signal orange. The shapes are square-cut rather than rounded, because the product belongs to metal shops, and one stroke always carries the accent.

Rules

Keep the orange stroke.

It is the only part that identifies the mark at 26 pixels. A single-colour version is not a Bacaan mark.

Clear space equals the tile's corner radius.

Nothing sits closer than 9px at logo size. Below 20px, drop the wordmark and use the tile alone.

Never stretch, rotate or recolour the strokes.

On a dark background the tile inverts to Fog and the strokes to Ink. The orange never changes.

No effects.

No gradients, no shadows on the mark, no outline versions. It is stamped, not drawn.

02 — Palette

Colour, and what each one is for

Click any colour to copy its value. Signal is for one thing at a time: the action you want taken. If two things on a screen are orange, one of them is wrong.

Status

These three are darker than a fill-only palette would suggest. They are used as small text as often as they are used as a dot or a bar — a status label, a percentage, a count — and the lighter values fell short of 4.5:1 on white. Darkening them also improves every chip that puts white text on top of them.

Colour never carries meaning alone. Every status shows a word next to it, because a fair number of people cannot tell the red from the green, and a phone screen in sunlight defeats the rest of us.

07 — Voice

How Bacaan sounds

Plain, calm and specific. The reader is at work and often in a hurry. Say what happened, then say what to do about it. No exclamation marks, no congratulating people for doing their job, and never a cheerful message about a failure.

Not this

Oops! Something went wrong 😬

This

The gateway refused the message. Nothing was sent.

Not this

Great job! You crushed it today! 🎉

This

Six workers clocked in. Two runs finished.

Not this

Optimise your operational throughput

This

See how far each job has got

Not this

Error 500

This

Something failed on our side. The fault has been logged and nothing you entered was lost.

Three rules

Name the thing. "Aluminium sheet is at zero", not "an inventory item requires attention".

Say what to do. Every warning carries one action and a link to the record that needs changing.

Admit the limits. If a number is an estimate, say so on the same line. Nobody trusts a system that has never been unsure.
