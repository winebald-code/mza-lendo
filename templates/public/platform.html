Everything a workshop actually runs on, and nothing it does not. Bacaan is deliberately narrow. It covers the five things that decide whether a plant makes money this month, connects them properly, and refuses to become a general-purpose ERP that nobody has time to fill in.

Manufacturing Pulse

One score, five subsystems, and the reason it moved. Each subsystem is scored from live records, then weighted by how fast it can stop a plant. A run that has slipped hurts more than a supplier who was two days late, so the weighting says so.

A score of 75 to 100 is healthy. From 50 to 74 needs attention. Below 50, something is wrong, and the dashboard says which subsystem moved and what to do about it.

Modules

Connected, so entering something once is enough.

  • Inventory. Materials with a minimum level and a reorder quantity. Every movement is written to a ledger with the balance after it, whether it came from the office or a handset.
  • Products and BOM. What you build and what one unit consumes. The bill of materials lets the plant work out shortages before a run starts, and consume stock when it finishes.
  • Orders. Customer orders that explode into dated production runs. Customers get an SMS at each status change, if they want one.
  • Production. Runs with stages the floor ticks off from their phone. A Gantt view shows two weeks at a glance and marks anything that has slipped.
  • Procurement. Purchase orders against suppliers, with partial receipts. Reliability is earned from what actually arrived and when, not typed in by hand.
  • Maintenance. Machines, faults and services. A critical fault stops the machine and texts the managers without anyone deciding to.
  • People. Workers, attendance and safety incidents, recorded from the floor rather than reconstructed at month end.

The same four keys on every screen

  • 0 goes back one step.
  • 00 goes to the main menu.
  • 98 shows the next page of a long list.
  • 99 stops and closes the session.

These are the keys Kenyan services already use, so nobody has to learn ours. They work on every screen. On a screen that asks for a number, 0 still means back, so you cannot type 0 as a quantity, which is not a useful thing to report anyway.

Workers can also just text the shortcode. TASKS, STOCK, IN, OUT and DOWN all work without opening a menu at all.
